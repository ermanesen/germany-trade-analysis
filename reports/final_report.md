# Germany Trade Exposure & Resilience

## Research question

How exposed is Germany to international trade relative to comparable EU economies, how has its external surplus changed since 2019, and do lagged trade conditions predict subsequent real GDP growth?

## Data and quality controls

The analysis uses three World Bank World Development Indicators for Germany and nine major EU benchmarks from 2000 through 2024: exports of goods and services (% of GDP), imports of goods and services (% of GDP), and annual real GDP growth. The committed snapshot contains every expected country-year combination: 10 countries × 25 years = 250 observations, with no duplicate keys or missing indicator values.

Trade balance is defined descriptively as exports minus imports, both expressed as shares of GDP. Trade openness is exports plus imports. These are accounting indicators, not causal treatments.

## Findings

Germany's trade surplus narrowed from 5.61% of GDP in 2019 to 3.78% in 2024, a decline of 1.83 percentage points. Exports fell 0.91 points while imports rose 0.92 points. Total trade openness was virtually unchanged at 79.09% of GDP, so the main change was composition rather than aggregate exposure.

Among the ten benchmark economies in 2024, Germany ranked fifth by trade balance, seventh by openness, third by export-import coverage, and ninth by GDP growth. Its openness was well below the nine-country peer median of 105.88%, while its balance remained above the peer median of 2.74%.

The 2001–2024 two-way fixed-effects model contains 240 observations. A one-point higher lagged trade balance is associated with a statistically imprecise 0.055-point reduction in subsequent growth (SE 0.051; p=0.305). A one-point higher lagged openness is associated with 0.031-point higher growth (SE 0.008; p=0.0029). The model uses country and year effects and country-clustered standard errors with small-cluster t inference.

## Interpretation

The evidence does not support treating a larger trade surplus as a reliable short-run growth signal. Germany's decision-relevant issue is the erosion of export coverage alongside weak recent growth, not trade integration by itself. A practical monitoring dashboard should therefore separate exposure (openness), position (balance and coverage), and outcome (growth).

## Limitations

The model is associative, not causal. Ten country clusters limit precision, and the annual macro panel omits exchange rates, energy prices, industrial composition, trading-partner demand, and supply-chain concentration. The World Bank indicators aggregate goods and services and cannot identify bilateral or sector-specific dependencies. Results should guide diagnosis, not policy counterfactuals.
